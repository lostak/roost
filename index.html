    Downline & override income

    Your downline is auto-detected from your commissions: any writing agent who ever appears at level (100) on one of your actual client policies is flagged as your downline (house/accounting lines like interest, bonuses and overrides don't count). Income here counts only pure-override policies — ones you did not personally co-write (the listed writing-agent levels already add up to 100). When you're on the split, the statement lumps your own cut in with the override, so that production can't be cleanly attributed and is left out. "Since" is when each agent first appeared.

    Downline hierarchy

    You at the top, your downline beneath (all at the same level by default). Drag an agent onto another to nest them if they're actually on that agent's downline; drop on You to move them back to the top. Each shows their downline count and override income.

    Adjust downline

    The (100) rule isn't perfect. Remove anyone it flagged by mistake, or add an agent it missed. Changes save to config.json and apply right away.

    Product detail

    Bonuses & overrides

    Trips, production bonuses, agency overrides and earnings adjustments show up in the statement's commission section but aren't recurring policy residual. Roost separates them here (and excludes them from your residual and income figures) so one-off payouts don't distort the trend. Note: some trip / 1099 lines are non-cash — booked as income and offset by a matching chargeback the same week (net $0) — so this total can exceed the actual bonus cash received.

    Rewrites — policies reissued, not lost

    When a clawback or a stopped residual is followed by a comparable product in the same line of business for the same client (e.g. a 10-year annuity reissued as a 15-year), the client stayed — it's a rewrite, not a true loss. A fresh initial commission offsets it. Residual lines that stop with no comparable replacement are counted as lapses.

    Review — data to check

    Roost flags statements that may not have parsed cleanly and pay periods that look missing, so a wrong or absent figure doesn't quietly skew your dashboard. Nothing here changes your numbers — it's a checklist. Re-upload a flagged statement, or drop a missing one in and hit Refresh.

    Organize files

    Statements are filed as <year>/M-D-YY.pdf from the date inside each PDF. If you dropped files in by hand or a file was named wrong (e.g. a Nov-2024 statement saved as 11-14-25.pdf), this renames and re-files them correctly. True duplicates are removed; nothing else is deleted. Preview first, then apply.

    Tax playbook for 1099 agents

    Figures use the latest finalized (2025) federal brackets, standard deduction, and Social Security wage base as an estimate; later tax years shift slightly with inflation. Insurance agents are not a specified service trade for QBI, so the 20% deduction applies regardless of income. This is an estimate to guide savings and quarterly payments — your CPA's return is authoritative.

    Cash flow & tax calendar

    What's coming in versus what you'll owe. Your expected residual income over the next 12 months, the quarterly estimated-tax due dates, and how much to bank each month so a tax bill never catches you short. Tax figures come from your Taxes estimate.

    The next 12 months

    Residual income is what your in-force book pays if it holds. "Bank for taxes" is a steady monthly set-aside; the reserve builds up and draws down as quarterly payments come due.

    Convention tracker — ASB 2027 incentive trip

    Qualification runs Feb 1, 2026 – Jan 31, 2027 (Nassau, Bahamas, Jun 10–14, 2027). Points are earned on new, issued annualized premium: Med Supp 125% (AARP/UHC flat 1,500), Medicare Advantage 2,000/policy, annuities 15% of premium, life & LTC / supplemental health 250%. Part D and under-65 medical don't count. You also need 25+ issued applications and at least 50% of credit from non-MA lines. Each level qualifies on downline OR personal production. These are Roost's estimates from your statements — ASB's official tally governs.

    Audit & reconciliation

    Trust checks on the numbers. Does your summed net match the printed year-to-date on the statement? Are there impossible YTD reversals (a sign of an off-sequence or duplicated statement), missing weeks, or residual payments that fell well below a policy's own norm — where a carrier may have shorted you?

    Year-to-date reconciliation

    Your summed pay-period net vs. the highest printed YTD on that year's statements. A gap means a statement carries income on a separate stream, or an off-sequence statement is throwing the running total. Click a year to see why.

    ⚠︎ Off-sequence statements (YTD went backwards)

    A printed year-to-date can't decrease. These statements are out of sequence or on a different stream — worth reconciling with the carrier.

    Missing weeks

    Gaps over two weeks between statements — usually a no-pay week, but confirm none went missing.

    Underpayment watch

    Residual policies whose most recent payment fell below half their own median — could be a wind-down, a rate change, or a carrier error to chase. (Medicare Advantage and life / final-expense excluded, since their first-year payout naturally steps down.)

    Clients & households

    Your book by person, not by policy. See who's worth the most, who has room for another product (a Medicare client with no dental or hospital plan is an easy conversation), and which clients are likely the same household.
